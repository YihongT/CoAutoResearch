# Human Factors and HRI Domain Pack

## Purpose

Apply human-factors and human-robot-interaction discipline whenever claims depend on human perception, behavior, performance, reliance, or experience. This pack does not replace ethics review, human-subject oversight, or a qualified domain specialist.

## Required framing

Record:

- participant population, inclusion and exclusion criteria, recruitment, sample size, and relevant experience;
- task, environment, exposure duration, interface, autonomy level, and human-system responsibility allocation;
- the construct being claimed and the exact measure used;
- comparison condition, timing, missing-data handling, and analysis boundary;
- accessibility, vulnerable-population, privacy, consent, and ethics-review posture where applicable.

## Construct and evidence discipline

- Keep perceived safety, objective safety, trust, confidence, comfort, acceptance, reliance, workload, situation awareness, usability, and performance distinct.
- State whether a measure is validated for the present population and context. If not, label it exploratory and justify the interpretation.
- Separate self-report, behavior, performance, physiological response, and system telemetry.
- Test or discuss learning, novelty, social desirability, demand characteristics, automation bias, mode confusion, and calibration.
- Evaluate who is excluded or underserved by the interface, environment, language, ability assumptions, or data collection.
- Preserve null, heterogeneous, adverse, and subgroup results when they qualify a human-facing claim.

## Claim limits

- Feeling safe is not evidence that a system is safe.
- High trust is not necessarily desirable; appropriate, calibrated reliance is the relevant target.
- Short-term laboratory acceptance is not durable real-world adoption.
- A single population, culture, task, device, or interface does not establish broad human generality.
- Automated reviewer agreement is not independent human-subject, ethics, or peer review.

## Missing expertise

If a claim requires psychometrics, experimental design, clinical assessment, accessibility, ethics, cultural analysis, or application-specific human-factors expertise not installed for the trial, record the missing specialization and narrow or block the claim.
