# Framing Move

## Use when

The highest-value uncertainty concerns problem definition, scope, contribution identity, claim hierarchy, target audience, venue posture, or which research line is defensible.

## Local question examples

- Which of two contribution frames best fits current evidence?
- Should a broad claim be narrowed after negative results?
- What scope boundary makes the current line defensible?
- Is the project currently a method, system, empirical, design, benchmark, or process contribution?

## Required work

1. State the framing uncertainty precisely.
2. Identify two or more plausible frames unless only one survives current evidence.
3. Map each frame to required evidence, likely venue fit, and failure conditions.
4. Select a working frame or record why no frame is viable.
5. State scope boundaries, non-goals, and claims that must be dropped or qualified.
6. Propose line/campaign changes through staging.

## Local completeness

The trial is complete when the specific framing question is resolved enough to guide the next move, or when the competing frames and human decision are sharply defined. It does not need to finish the entire paper architecture.

## Result cards

Typical cards: claim revision, design decision, boundary condition, negative evidence, venue decision.

## Do not

- rewrite the whole manuscript merely because framing changed;
- ignore inconvenient results;
- select a frame only because it sounds more impressive;
- change a locked venue or project identity without human authority.
